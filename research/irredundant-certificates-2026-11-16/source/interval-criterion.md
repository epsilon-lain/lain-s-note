External baseline: external/positive-edge-general-m@1, fixed README fully seed-read, already proves that a forest with component sizes A=(a_1,...,a_k), sum A=2m, completes to two m-cycles iff m is a subset sum of A, and that the optimal single-cycle positive-edge certificate cost is m. These are not native discoveries. Native extension: such S is inclusion-minimal iff m is not a subset sum of A and for every i with a_i>1, SS(A without a_i) contains [m-a_i+1,m-1]. Proof: deleting the edge in position t of a path with a_i vertices replaces a_i by t,a_i-t. Since A has no subset sum m, a newly formed m-subset must use exactly one piece. This exists iff SS(A without a_i) contains m-t or m-a_i+t. The two alternatives are equivalent by complements in the remaining total 2m-a_i. Thus the deletion destroys certification iff m-t is attainable. Quantify t=1,...,a_i-1. Singleton components have no edge deletion test. Necessity and sufficiency follow. Every integer partition of 2m can be realized by consecutive path components on the fixed target cycle. For k parts of multiplicities r_d, its number of fixed-cycle edge subsets is 2m*(k-1)!/product_d(r_d!): count a chosen starting vertex and an ordered part list, then divide by k possible component starts. Therefore all partitions plus interval tests give the complete cost spectrum 2m-k. Native exhaustive computation: all partitions of 2m for 3<=m<=20; compare interval against literal every-split for m<=12. Separate-in-method direct candidate enumeration at m=3,4 covers all 70/2835 labeled candidates and all 64/256 target-edge subsets; every certificate and minimality test agrees. Same author wrote both methods, so this is not independent review. Exact examples: m6 costs {6,8}, not 7; m12 costs {12,17,18}; m20 costs {20,26,29,30,31,32,33,34}, largest witness parts (2,6,6,7,9,10). m4 reproduces old {4,5}, not new discovery. Finite spectra alone imply no theorem for untested m.
