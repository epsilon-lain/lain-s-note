Write SS(A) for subset sums of an integer multiset A of total 2h. Call A critical if it has no sum h, but splitting any part a into t,a-t for every 1<=t<a creates a sum h. By M-05ac7d1d25ae1353@1, these exactly encode inclusion-minimal positive-edge certificates on a single target cycle (proper edge subsets). The completion criterion and optimal cost m are the external baseline, not new native discoveries.

EXTENSION LEMMA. Suppose A is critical for h and SS(A) contains both [h-L,h-1] and [h+1,h+L], with integers 1<=L<=h. Let b=2L+1>h. Then A' = A union {b,b} is critical for H=h+b and SS(A') contains [H-2L,H-1] and [H+1,H+2L]. Proof of no sum H: zero new b-parts give at most 2h<H; two give at least 2b>H; exactly one would need the excluded old sum h. For deleting/splitting an old part, old criticality supplies an h-subset, and adding one b gives H. For a new b-part, its remaining parts are A union {b}. Their subset sums include [h+1,h+L] from A and b+[h-L,h-1]=[h+L+1,h+b-1], so cover the entire interval [H-b+1,H-1]; the interval criterion proves every split destroys certification. For near-H coverage, b+[h+1,h+L]=[H+1,H+L] and 2b+[h-L,h-1]=[H+L+1,H+2L] cover the upper interval. Complementation in total 2H gives the lower interval. This proves all assertions.

EXPLICIT FAMILY. For any integer r>=1, start with A_0=(1 repeated r-1 times, r+1), half h_0=r, width L_0=r. Its subset sums are exactly [0,r-1] union [r+1,2r]; it is critical because the sole nontrivial part r+1 has remaining subset sums [0,r-1], exactly its split interval. This is an integer lemma even for r=1,2; the final graph family always has m>=3. For j=1,...,s append two copies of b_j=2^j*r+1. By induction h_j=(2^(j+1)-1)*r+j and L_j=2^j*r. At step j, b_j-h_(j-1)=r+2-j>0 for 1<=j<=r+1, so the extension lemma applies. Thus for every 1<=s<=r+1, A_s is critical and has k=r+2s parts.

Choose s=r+1. Then m_r=h_(r+1)=r*2^(r+2)+1, k_r=3r+2, and the component multiset is (1^(r-1), r+1, and two copies of 2^j*r+1 for each 1<=j<=r+1). Any prescribed cyclic order realizes it by contiguous observed paths. The resulting certificate has cost 2m_r-3r-2, whereas the minimum is m_r by the adopted external theorem. Hence ratio = 2-(3r+2)/(r*2^(r+2)+1), tending to 2. For example r=5 gives m=641, k=17, cost1265 versus optimum641.

GREEDY CONSEQUENCE. Any such inclusion-minimal S can occur as output of redundancy-only deletion from the full target edge set: first delete edges outside S; every intermediate set contains the certificate S, so remains a certificate. At S no edge can be deleted. Thus no fixed c<2 bounds the worst-case approximation ratio of arbitrary-order redundancy deletion in this model. Conversely, every inclusion-minimal certificate is a proper subset of the 2m target edges (the full cycle is not minimal), so its cost is <=2m-1<2m. The supremum of this worst-case ratio is exactly 2, not an attained ratio2 and not a hardness result for all algorithms.

COMPONENT COUNT BOUND. For any critical partition of 2m into k parts, the interval criterion forces SS(A without a_i) to contain a_i-1 distinct integers. There are at most 2^(k-1) subsets of k-1 parts, so a_i-1<=2^(k-1), including the trivial case a_i=1. Summing gives 2m<=k*(2^(k-1)+1). This supplies a logarithmic-order obstruction to extremely few components; the explicit family uses only O(log m) components along its infinite parameter sequence. No formula for the exact largest irredundant cost for all m is claimed.
