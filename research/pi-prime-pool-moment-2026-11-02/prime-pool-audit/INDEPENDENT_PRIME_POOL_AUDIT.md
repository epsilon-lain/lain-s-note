# Independent audit: midpoint removal and mean root capacity

2026-11-02. This is an internal independent proof and exact-arithmetic audit. It does **not** prove the sufficient global capacity bound for the active prime pool.

Fix U_0=1, U_1=2 and

\[
(n+1)U_{n+1}=2(2n+1)U_n+4nU_{n-1}.
\]

For an odd prime p, let h=(p-1)/2,

\[
z_p=\#\{0\le r<p:U_r\equiv0\pmod p\},\quad
\epsilon_p=1_{p\mid U_h},\quad
k_p=\#\{0\le r<h:U_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

Here k_p is a root count, not a valuation cap from the earlier checkpoint. Let Z be the set of primes for which z_p>0, and define

\[
R(x)=\sum_{p\le x,\ p\text{ odd}}z_p\ln p,\qquad
T(x)=\sum_{p\le x,\ p\text{ odd}}\epsilon_p\ln p.
\]

## 1. The exact counting inequality

Holt reflection U_(p-1-r)=(-4)^(h-r)U_r modulo p pairs every nonmidpoint zero with its distinct reflection. Its factor is a unit, hence

\[
z_p=2k_p+\epsilon_p.
\]

If epsilon_p=0 and p is active, k_p>=1; if epsilon_p=1, the midpoint itself makes p active. Thus, prime by prime,

\[
1_Z(p)\le\frac{z_p+\epsilon_p}{2},
\quad\text{and therefore}\quad
\boxed{\vartheta_Z(x)\le\tfrac12R(x)+\tfrac12T(x).}
\tag{1}
\]

One must keep T(x) at this stage. At a fixed approximation index n there is at most one exact midpoint prime, but across all primes this alone does not make T(x) negligible.

## 2. The fixed midpoint curve and its trace

The midpoint coefficient belongs to the fixed curve

\[
E:y^2=x(x^2+2x+2),\qquad\Delta=-256.
\]

For a good odd prime p, write a_p=p+1-#E(F_p). The coefficient identity and Euler's criterion give

\[
a_p\equiv [x^{p-1}]\{x(x^2+2x+2)\}^{h}=U_h\pmod p.
\]

For p>=5, Hasse's bound |a_p|<=2sqrt(p)<p implies epsilon_p=1 exactly when a_p=0. The exceptional small prime p=3 has U_1=2 and epsilon_3=0, so it does not affect the asymptotic conclusion.

## 3. Independent non-CM check by two ordinary reductions

Direct point counts give

| p | #E(F_p) | a_p | a_p^2-4p | Q(Frobenius) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 2 | 2 | -8 | Q(sqrt(-2)) |
| 5 | 8 | -2 | -16 | Q(i) |

Both reductions are good, since p does not divide -256, and ordinary, since p does not divide a_p.

Suppose E had geometric complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field K. Good reduction injects geometric characteristic-zero endomorphisms into geometric endomorphisms of the reduction (after a finite number-field extension on which they are defined). For an ordinary reduction, the geometric endomorphism algebra equals the imaginary quadratic field generated by Frobenius. Consequently K would embed into each of the two fields in the table. Because all have dimension two over Q, these embeddings are isomorphisms. But Q(sqrt(-2)) and Q(i) are not isomorphic as Q-fields. This contradiction proves that E is non-CM.

This argument uses geometric, not merely F_p-rational, endomorphisms. The ordinary hypothesis is essential: for a supersingular curve the geometric algebra is quaternionic, even when the F_p-rational algebra is quadratic.

## 4. Unconditional removal of midpoint prime mass

Serre (1981), Theorem 20 and Corollary 2, apply to any non-CM elliptic curve over Q. In particular the number of good primes p<=x with a_p=0 is o(x/ln x), unconditionally. For this fixed E, therefore,

\[
\boxed{T(x)=o(x),}
\]

because T(x) is at most ln(x) times that prime count. No Lang--Trotter conjecture or GRH is used for this qualitative consequence.

Substituting into (1) proves the audited capacity reduction

\[
\boxed{\vartheta_Z(x)\le\tfrac12R(x)+o(x).}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus a sufficient next target is: for some fixed eta>0 and every sufficiently large x,

\[
\boxed{R(x)\le(1+\tfrac12\ln2-\eta)x.}
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed (2) gives theta_Z(x)<=(1/2+ln2/4-eta/2)x+o(x). The preceding multi-band theorem then yields ln Q_n>=2eta n+o(n), which tends to infinity. The slack is preserved by taking a slightly smaller positive constant if an eventual bound with no o(x) term is desired.

The target (3), whose coefficient before subtraction is 1.3465735902..., is **not proved here**. The local reflection only supplies the factor one-half; it gives no cross-prime upper bound on the weighted mean root count R(x).

## 5. Two equivalent exact finite-field formulations

The generating function of U_n is (1-4z-4z^2)^(-1/2). In characteristic p its first p coefficients coincide with those of

\[
(1-4z-4z^2)^{(p-1)/2}.
\tag{4}
\]

For example, this follows from the Frobenius identity

L(z)=(1-4z-4z^2)^{(p-1)/2}L(z^p)

in F_p[[z]]. Thus Z is exactly the set of primes for which the p-dependent quadratic power in (4) has a zero coefficient.

Let chi be the quadratic character of F_p, with chi(0)=0. Finite-field power sums show, for 1<=r<=p-1,

\[
\boxed{U_r\equiv-\sum_{a\in F_p}a^{p-1-r}\chi(1-4a-4a^2)\pmod p.}
\tag{5}
\]

The case r=0 is excluded because an additional highest-degree coefficient contributes. A character-sum size bound alone does not prove nonvanishing modulo p; in particular an upper bound on absolute value is compatible with exact zero.

Equivalently, if i^2=-1 in F_(p^2), then U_r=(-2i)^r P_r(i), with P_r the Legendre polynomial. For general r/p the degree varies with p; the midpoint identification with one fixed elliptic curve cannot be applied silently to all r.

## 6. Exact bounded checks and sources

`verify_prime_pool_independent.py` uses only Python's standard library and no author scanning module. Through p<=503 it checks the full coefficient identity, reflection, root-pair counts and midpoint trace congruence using independently expanded polynomials and direct point counts. The character-moment identity is checked through p<=127. It also verifies the two small ordinary reductions and their distinct squarefree Frobenius discriminants. This audits the algebra and implementation; it does not computationally prove a density theorem or (3).
