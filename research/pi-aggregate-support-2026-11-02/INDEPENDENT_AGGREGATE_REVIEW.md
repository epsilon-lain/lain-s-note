# Independent audit of the aggregate period polynomial certificates

The replacement theorem, the complete six-row cancellation calculation, and the 32-row polynomial and Sturm certificates pass this audit. The standard-library verifier was rerun successfully. Additional checks used independent SymPy matrix determinants and Sturm routines. The conclusions remain qualitative: none supplies the missing determinant-size estimate.

## 1. Cramer's rule for one replacement

Assume \(C_{t-1}=C_t=B\), and write

\[
A_Z(X)=\prod_{j=0}^{K-1}(X-jZ)^B,\qquad
X^{KB}=A_Z(X)+R_Z(X),\quad\deg R_Z<KB.
\]

The term \((Y-1)^{t-1}R_Z(X)\) is a combination of the existing columns at level \(t-1\). Subtracting it from the proposed new column does not change that column's coordinate along the deleted level-\(t\) column.

After subtraction, the new column is \((Y-1)^{t-1}A_Z(X)\). Every source row of time below \(t-1\) vanishes by the time factor. At time \(t-1\), all types \(\beta<B\) vanish because \(A_Z\) has a zero of order \(B\) at every center. At time \(t\), coefficient extraction gives precisely the ordinary Hermite data of \(A'_Z\). The next coefficient of the time factor only multiplies the already vanishing Hermite data of \(A_Z\).

Because the complete staircase matrix is block lower triangular with invertible time blocks, these zero lower-time data imply zero coordinates at every level below \(t\). At level \(t\), the coordinates are the monomial coefficients of \(A'_Z\). Its leading coefficient is \(KB\), so the coordinate along the deleted column \((Y-1)^tX^{KB-1}\) is exactly \(KB\).

Cramer's rule therefore gives \(P_1(Z)=KB\,P_0(Z)\) with the inserted column kept in the deleted column's position. This proof is valid for each nonzero \(Z\). Since both sides are polynomials, the identity extends to all \(Z\). The time surplus rises by one, and the spatial degree surplus also rises by one. The ordinary-coefficient normalization introduces no extra factorial into the multiplier.

## 2. Multiple replacements, budget, and all-height availability

Before subtracting the remainder, a replacement associated with time \(t_j\) has zero evaluations, and hence zero original staircase coordinates, at every time below \(t_j-1\). If \(t_{j+1}-t_j\ge2\), every earlier deleted position lies below that threshold for every later replacement. The replacement-coordinate matrix at the deleted positions is consequently triangular, with diagonal \(KB_i\). Its determinant is the product of those entries. Off-diagonal coefficients at later times do not affect this conclusion.

The new columns are distinct and are absent from the original staircase: at their new level \(t_i-1\), the exponent \(KB_i\) is exactly one above the old maximum. The separation condition also avoids an earlier deleted position being a potentially interfering remainder coordinate.

The original bound is

\[
w_0t+w_1\alpha<\eta H+(K-1)w_1,
\qquad \eta=\max(w_0/v_0,Kw_1/v_1)<1.
\]

A replacement changes the cost by \(w_1-w_0\), so its cost is less than \(\eta H+Kw_1-w_0\). The stated sufficient condition \(H>Kw_1/(1-\eta)\) therefore fits every original and replaced column into the budget.

The assertion of at least one replacement for **every** sufficiently large height uses the additional stated condition \(v_1\ge2v_0\), not merely the two strict budget inequalities. To check it, let

\[
t_{\max}=\lceil H/v_0\rceil-1,\qquad
\delta=H-v_0t_{\max}\in(0,v_0].
\]

When \(H>v_0\), both final time levels exist. Their residual heights are \(\delta\) and \(\delta+v_0\), both in \((0,v_1]\). Hence both ceilings equal one. Without an availability assumption or an explicit flat pair, the replacement identity is conditional on finding such a pair.

For fixed weights, there are only \(O(H)\) available time levels, so \(r=O(H)\), while the complete source has \(M_H=\Theta(H^2)\). The normalized logarithmic contribution of the explicit factor \((1-\zeta)^r\) therefore vanishes for fixed \(\zeta\ne1\), as stated. This scale comparison does not assert anything for an uncontrolled \(H\)-dependent error parameter.

## 3. Interpolation gives exact polynomials, not fitted approximations

After multiplying a row by its time factorial, every entry is an integer polynomial in the formal center \(Z\). Its center degree is at most \(\alpha-\beta\). Every nonzero determinant permutation therefore has degree at most

\[
D=\sum_C\alpha-\sum_L\beta.
\]

Evaluating at \(D+1\) distinct integer centers and interpolating recovers the determinant polynomial exactly. The two extra evaluation points in the verifier are useful consistency checks; uniqueness already follows from the degree bound. Dividing by the product of time factorials correctly restores ordinary coefficient jets.

The code's forward-difference basis is \(\binom Zk\), generated by multiplication by \((Z-k)/(k+1)\), so its rational interpolation normalization is correct. The integer Bareiss routine checks every exact division. The verifier is self-contained apart from its accompanying finite column fixture.

The rerun confirms the complete six-row sum: 36 target choices, 11 nonzero target minors, and aggregate polynomial \(Z^3(Z+3)-3Z^3\zeta\). The six nonzero degree-two contributions cancel exactly, while higher-degree target minors are individually zero in this example.

## 4. The 32-row certificate and exact root count

For both raw and shifted versions, the rerun gives

\[
D=62,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_0P_C=27,\qquad\deg P_C=43,
\]

and the selected-target polynomial is exactly \(-1697809Z^{32}/147\). The displayed raw degree-30 coefficient was independently compared coefficient by coefficient with the saved rational polynomial and agrees exactly.

As an additional check independent of the verifier's Stirling recurrence and Bareiss implementation, the factorial-scaled source matrices were reconstructed at \(Z=-2\) using direct binomial expansion of \((Y-1)^h\). SymPy's domain-based exact determinant agrees with the interpolated polynomial in both bases. This center was not among the verifier's interpolation or supplementary samples.

For \(R_C=P_C/Z^{27}\), independent SymPy rational calculations confirm in both bases:

\[
\deg R_C=16,\qquad\deg\gcd(R_C,R'_C)=0,
\qquad V(-\infty)=9,\quad V(+\infty)=7.
\]

Thus there are exactly two real roots and fourteen nonreal roots. The standard-library Sturm implementation preserves signs when clearing denominators and contents, because all normalization factors are positive. Its final nonzero constant confirms squarefreeness. The reciprocal polynomial inherits the nonzero reciprocal roots of \(R_C\), including all fourteen nonreal roots; any additional zero-root factor does not remove them.

The aggregate degree is \(62-27=35\). The coefficient at degree 30 is a nonzero polynomial in the actual transcendental period, so it is genuinely nonzero there. This is a statement about the complete unmarked error coefficient. It does not identify the selected 22-outside-row term as individually surviving, or establish the coefficient of an outside-row marker.

The verifier also confirms exact nonvanishing at the rational center \(44i/7\) by rational real and imaginary components. Its final 56-row two-replacement example returns \(P_0=Z^{72}\), \(P_2=8Z^{72}\), and the aggregate factor \((1-\zeta)^2\), consistent with the general Cramer proof.

**Audit outcome:** the stated algebraic and root-count conclusions are supported. The all-height availability condition, fixed-error scale comparison, and distinction between error degree and outside-row count must be retained. No magnitude estimate or critical high-dimensional Diophantine conclusion follows from these certificates alone.
