# A finite global optimum with a direct marginal certificate

This is an exact finite structural result. It does not verify the manuscript's unknown separated-weight thresholds or an all-large-
\(H\) claim. No rank-deficient modular specialization is used as a characteristic-zero upper bound.

Take
\[
m=2,\quad H=8,\quad (w_1,w_2)=(1,5),\quad
w_0=1,\quad v_0=\tfrac12,\quad K=2,\quad \theta=\tfrac14,
\qquad c=\frac{\log2}{4},\quad \nu=2.
\]
There are 48 original rows and 55 columns. For each \(j=0,1\), the rows are
\[
\beta=(0,0),\quad 0\le s\le15;
\qquad \beta=(1,0),\quad 0\le s\le7.
\]
All \(j=0\) rows are fixed at \(a=\beta\). The other 24 rows may choose **any**
\(a\ge\beta\) with \(a_1+5a_2\le8\), with \(\ell=s\) and \(d=k=0\).
There are 13 possible types before imposing \(a\ge\beta\).

We minimize the exact cost
\[
C=\frac{2\sum_{\text{rows}}w(a-\beta)+c\sum_a n_a^2}{MH},
\qquad MH=384.
\]

## An explicit feasible optimum

Write \(a_r=(r,0)\). Keep all anchor rows \(j=0\) fixed. For \(j=1\), use

| Original row | Chosen type |
|---|---|
| \(\beta=(1,0),\ 0\le s\le7\) | \(a_2\) |
| \(\beta=(0,0),\ 0\le s\le4\) | \(a_0\) |
| \(\beta=(0,0),\ 5\le s\le11\) | \(a_1\) |
| \(\beta=(0,0),\ s=12\) | \(a_2\) |
| \(\beta=(0,0),\ 13\le s\le15\) | \(a_3\) |

Every row meets \(a\ge\beta\) and \(wa\le H\). All labels \((j,s,a)\) are distinct.
The free-row counts are \((5,7,9,3)\), and the total type counts, including the
anchor counts \((16,8,0,0)\), are
\[
(n_{a_0},n_{a_1},n_{a_2},n_{a_3})=(21,15,9,3).
\]
Thus
\[
2\sum w(a-\beta)=2(7+18+9-8)=52,
\qquad \sum n_a^2=21^2+15^2+9^2+3^2=756,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{C_{\min}=\frac{13}{96}+\frac{63}{32}c
=\frac{13}{96}+\frac{63\log2}{128}.}
\]

## Why this is the global minimum over all 13 candidate types

Drop the restrictions tying a free type to its original \(\beta\), drop distinct-label
requirements, and drop all rank requirements. This relaxation can only lower the
minimum. The original free rows have total \(\sum w\beta=8\), so their contribution
to the numerator, up to the constant \(-16+c(16^2+8^2)\), is obtained by assigning
24 units to types.

The marginal cost of the \(k\)-th free unit of type \(a\) is
\[
\Delta_a(k)=2wa+c\bigl(2n_a^{\rm anchor}+2k-1\bigr),\qquad k\ge1.
\]
These marginal costs are strictly increasing in \(k\). The proposed counts choose
the first 5, 7, 9, and 3 marginals respectively for \(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3\), and no
marginals for any other type. This is a set of 24 prefix-closed marginals.

| Type | Last selected marginal | First unselected marginal |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(a_0\) | \(41c\) | \(43c\) |
| \(a_1\) | \(2+29c\) | \(2+31c\) |
| \(a_2\) | \(4+17c\) | \(4+19c\) |
| \(a_3\) | \(6+5c\) | \(6+7c\) |
| Every other allowed type | none | at least \(8+c\) |

The last line covers **all** remaining candidates: any allowed type other than
\((0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0)\) has weight at least 4 and anchor multiplicity zero.
Later marginals for every type are larger than the first unselected marginal.

For \(1/6<c<3/17\), the largest selected marginal is \(41c\): its differences
from the other three last-selected values are respectively
\(12c-2\), \(24c-4\), and \(36c-6\), all positive.
The smallest unselected marginal is \(6+7c\): the differences from it of the
other three first-unselected values are \(36c-6\), \(24c-4\), and \(12c-2\),
all positive; and \((8+c)-(6+7c)=2-6c>0\).
Finally,
\[
41c<6+7c\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c<3/17.
\]
Therefore every selected marginal is strictly cheaper than every unselected one.
The proposed counts are the unique optimal **aggregate counts** in the relaxed
problem. The displayed feasible row assignment attains the same lower bound in
the original problem, proving global optimality. This argument uses neither
exhaustive rank testing nor modular rank-deficiency pruning.

The required interval follows elementarily from
\[
\log2=2\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{1}{(2k+1)3^{2k+1}}.
\]
Keeping the first term gives \(\log2>2/3\). Replacing every omitted denominator
\(2k+1\) by 3 gives the strict upper bound
\[
\log2<\frac23+\frac23\sum_{k\ge1}3^{-(2k+1)}
=\frac{25}{36}.
\]
Hence \(1/6<c<25/144<3/17\).

Using the column ordering generated by the local `matrix_core.py`, the

with the local `matrix_core.py` and independent `verify.py` returned, modulo

Both are nonzero. The first certifies a nonzero polynomial minor over
\(\mathbb Q[W]\); using the known transcendence of \(\pi\), the same selected
minor is nonzero at \(W=2\pi i\). The second certifies the original rational-center
minor on the **same monomial columns**. As in the existing fixtures, choosing
\(F_0=17\) gives \(T_i=34w_i>15\), so the required original \(G_i\) jets equal
the logarithm jets used in this calculation. These are positive modular
certificates only; no modular vanishing is interpreted as a polynomial identity.

For comparison, the earlier finite assignment had cost
\(25/192+(33/16)c\). The new cost is strictly smaller by
\[
\frac{3c}{32}-\frac{1}{192}>0.
\]
The aggregate minimum and this explicit full-rank witness together prove a
global finite optimum over all admissible choices of \(a\), within the fixed
\(\ell=s,d=k=0,j=0\) identity scope.
